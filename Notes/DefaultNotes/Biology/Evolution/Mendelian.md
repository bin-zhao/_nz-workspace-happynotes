What is Mendelian?

"Mendelian" is an adjective that refers to anything related to <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">the principles of heredity discovered by Gregor Mendel</span>. An Augustinian friar and scientist, Mendel is considered the "father of modern genetics" for his<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)"> groundbreaking experiments with pea plants in <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">the 19th century</span>. 
</span>
Key concepts of Mendelian genetics

The term is most often used in the phrase "Mendelian inheritance," which encompasses three key principles: 

- **Law of Segregation:** Alleles for a single trait separate during the formation of gametes (sex cells) so that each gamete carries only one allele for that gene.
- **Law of Independent Assortment:** Alleles for different traits are sorted into gametes independently of one another, provided the genes are on different chromosomes.
- **Law of Dominance:** Some alleles (the dominant ones) will mask the effect of others (the recessive ones) in the phenotype (observable traits). 

Mendelian traits

A "Mendelian trait" is a characteristic determined by a single gene at a single location on a chromosome. Examples include some single-gene diseases like cystic fibrosis and Huntington's disease, as well as simpler traits like the rhesus blood antigen. Many traits, however, are **non-Mendelian** and are influenced by multiple genes or environmental factors.
