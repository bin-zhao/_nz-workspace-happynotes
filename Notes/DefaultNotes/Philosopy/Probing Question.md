A **probing question** is a **follow-up question designed to explore something more deeply** — to get beyond surface answers and uncover details, reasoning, emotions, or motives.

### 1. **Definition**

> A *probing question* is a question that seeks clarification, elaboration, or evidence from someone’s previous statement.
> It encourages the speaker to think more carefully and express what they *really mean*.

### 2. **Purpose**

Probing questions are used to:

* Clarify vague or incomplete answers
* Reveal underlying causes or reasoning
* Encourage reflection and critical thinking
* Test accuracy or consistency

They are common in **teaching**, **interviewing**, **therapy**, and **investigation**.

### 3. **Examples**

| Surface Question                 | Probing Follow-up                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| “Do you like your job?”          | “What parts of your job do you find most satisfying?” |
| “That project failed.”           | “Why do you think it didn’t work out?”                |
| “The movie was good.”            | “What exactly made it good in your opinion?”          |
| “I felt upset.”                  | “Can you tell me what made you feel that way?”        |
| “I think this method is better.” | “What evidence supports that conclusion?”             |

Notice how each probing question **pushes for explanation or justification**.

### 4. **Types of probing questions**

* **Clarifying** – “What do you mean by that?”
* **Reasoning** – “Why do you believe that’s true?”
* **Evidence-based** – “Can you give an example?”
* **Consequence-based** – “What would happen if we didn’t do that?”
* **Reflective** – “How did that experience change your view?”

### 5. **Comparison**

| Type                 | Goal                    | Example                     |
| -------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Closed question**  | Get a fact              | “Did you finish?”           |
| **Open question**    | Get general info        | “How did it go?”            |
| **Probing question** | Get depth and reasoning | “What made it go that way?” |

### 6. **Usage note**

A good probing question is:

* **Neutral**, not accusatory (“Can you explain?” ≠ “Why didn’t you think?”)
* **Specific**, not vague (“What happened next?” is better than “Tell me more.”)
